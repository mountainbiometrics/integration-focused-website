# MTN Guide — Engineering Reference for Messaging

**Purpose.** This memo records how MTN Guide actually works at the engineering level, so website and marketing copy can be grounded in what is built rather than in strategy decks. It exists because the site lags the product, and because the architecture's central promise — calibrated trust — is the one thing a careless capability claim destroys.

**Sources.** Two, and they disagree in places worth knowing about.

1. **Sinai** (`../sinai/kb/`, wired to this repo by `.live_docs.toml`) — MTN's live_docs store: ~956 atomic design docs, 109 raw clippings, 156 reviews. This is the authoritative engineering record. Doc IDs below are Sinai timestamps; read them at `../sinai/kb/02-docs/<id>.md`. The `ldoc` CLI is not installed locally — read the files directly and walk `belongs_to` links.
2. **Notion** — strategy, sales calls, grant material, design briefs. Carries positioning and market direction, not build state.

**Status.** Written 2026-08-27, against Sinai as of its 2026-08-17 docs and Notion through 2026-08-27. Everything here has a drift half-life; re-verify against Sinai before shipping copy that depends on it.

**Depth warning.** This is deliberately deeper than any messaging will be. The point is that whoever writes the shallow version knows what they are simplifying.

## 2. The operating premise

Everything downstream follows from one stated premise (`20260701230700`): **evidence about any source is always partial, multi-modal, and possibly stale — and that is the reason the product exists, not a gap to eliminate.**

The distilled engineering principles (`20260707181842`) restate it as corollary 12: *imperfection is the domain, not a defect.* Partial, messy, siloed data **is** the problem space, so the system is designed and tested against realistic simulated messiness rather than pristine samples.

Two neighbouring corollaries carry unusual weight for copy:

- **"Never dress an assumption as a fact."** Deterministically-derived facts and heuristic guesses are separated, and guesses carry explicit confidence. The surprising null and the oddly-nested field are exactly where the system must stop rather than skip.
- **"Map, don't merge."** A single perfect unified ontology cannot resolve genuine semantic conflict between departments. The aim is a map — equivalence, overlap, contradiction — built per-source, not a forced consolidation.

That second one is the honest one-sentence version of the whole product.

## 3. Architecture

Four parts.

### The hub (Elixir, `field_map` repo)

One API, one write path, one contract. Every mutation flows through it — a pipeline capability writing live over MCP, a human correction through the UI, an imported job artifact. MCP servers, HTTP surfaces, and the UI are **adapters over the single internal API**; nothing gets a private path into persistence or orchestration, so machine and human callers share identical semantics (`20260708191516`).

The scope discipline is explicit — the docs call it the mud test (`20260708191332`): the hub persists, routes jobs, and serves. It holds **no enrichment logic and no semantic judgment**. Those live in the pipeline (machine) or with the reviewing human. A hub stays a hub exactly as long as this holds.

Why one hub: the map's most valuable content is non-recomputable. A pipeline output can be regenerated by re-running the job; human-provided evidence and corrections cannot.

### Persistence — an EAV/triple store

The map persists in Postgres as entities + triples + annotations-as-triples-about-triples, with domain models as Ecto embedded schemas assembled over the substrate (`20260710202417`). Critiqued at forest level and deliberately **reaffirmed** on 2026-07-10 over a relational column-per-field model.

The rationale is that per-fact provenance and confidence are product requirements that force property reification in *any* design — column-per-field arrives at half-EAV without the uniformity — and that the property vocabulary evolves continuously, which EAV absorbs with no migration.

What makes this doc worth reading is that the **weaknesses are recorded alongside the decision rather than hidden**: the database cannot enforce domain integrity, values travel as text so type errors surface at assembly, predicate typos are silent, ad-hoc filtered queries over triples are join gymnastics, and list reads carry N+1 assembly risk. Integrity moves to the application write path. This is characteristic of how the whole store is written and is itself a credibility asset if we ever talk about engineering culture.

### Layers — the recursive scoping primitive

`20260720191248`. Two kinds:

- a **source layer** — a layer for one data source, holding its directly-ingested artifacts and parsed evidence; the consistency boundary those artifacts must agree within
- a **composite layer** — composed of source layers and/or other composite layers

No fixed taxonomy of tiers (system / company / etc. were considered and rejected). How many levels exist and what they mean is the customer's structural choice.

Every entity, triple, document, and event lives in exactly **one** layer. A layer's *scope* — itself plus, transitively, its components — is what entity identity, write access, and reference reachability are all defined against.

Two real problems dissolved when scoping became recursive: the canonical/common data model had no home and would have been a special orphaned object; and two representations of the same underlying data (a DB schema and its API projection) could not be held apart — ingested into one source they would wrongly merge under natural-key identity, yet they are not different systems either. Now the canonical model is an ordinary composite layer's concept layer, and same-data representations are sibling source layers reconciled by the composite above them.

**Maintenance consequence, and this is the customer-visible one:** when a source changes, only the edge connecting that source layer to its composite needs re-evaluation. The rest of the map is untouched. This is both the maintenance argument and the LLM-accuracy argument — scoping a change to one edge gives the model a far smaller problem than re-evaluating an estate.

### The annotation pipeline (Python)

Deterministic structural derivation runs first — one Concept per table, one ConceptAttribute per column, FK-derived relationships — then orthogonal enrichment capabilities add semantics over that skeleton. The seam between deterministic derivation and semantic enrichment is the load-bearing architectural divide (`20260622214101`).

Capabilities read and write the map **live** through the hub's MCP surface; there is no local execution mode (`20260716044405`). The pipeline repo owns no map state.

## 4. The data model — assertion-evidence

ADR-002 (`20260716210241`). The design is owner-ratified; the implementation details within it are explicitly marked revisable. Five things matter:

**Existence is itself an assertion.** Creating a concept assigns the id and records an `existence: asserted` assertion through the same write path as any value — linked to its Event, scored, citing evidence. It is multi-assertable, so re-deriving a concept **corroborates** rather than erroring. Creating an already-existing identity appends a corroborating existence assertion; per-item results are `created` vs `corroborated`.

**One general evidence mechanism.** Every assertion cites what it rests on through a single `evidence: [ids]` reference, validated per item to Evidence-family entities or chunks **within the writing layer's scope** (`20260716210406`). A source layer may cite only its own evidence; a composite layer's alignment assertions may cite down into its components. Three earlier mechanisms — derivation edges, value assertions, chunk citations — collapsed into this one. A third citation kind completes it: a **user citation** records a human's own judgment as material evidence. The confirmation itself is evidence, identified by the person.

**Provenance is computed, never stored.** An assertion stores only its producing Event reference; the tier is a pure function of that Event's actor and mode — deterministic run → `schema_derived`, LLM run → `model_suggested`, human action → `human_confirmed`.

**Confidence is universal.** Every assertion carries `self_reported_confidence`. LLM writers score explicitly; deterministic and human writes default to 1.0 — because a deterministic assertion is certain, and that certainty is itself the signal.

**The evidence set is part of the assertion's identity** (`20260728211836`). A duplicate assertion of the same value records only when its evidence differs from every identical assertion already standing — that difference is what makes it an independent corroborating observation. Competing values always record. Disagreement between writers is held as the **plain coexistence of their claims**, not resolved and not marked with a special term.

### Cross-layer correspondence

A claim that a concept in one layer and a concept in another refer to overlapping subject matter is a **typed relationship entity with its own identity**, written into the composite layer enclosing both (`20260811215553`). Where it lives is forced rather than chosen: references reach only within the writing layer's scope, so the smallest layer containing both endpoints is the composite that composes them.

And it is never a bare equals sign (`20260811215522`). What one system calls a customer and another calls a customer usually share most of their extent and disagree at the edges, and **whether the disagreement matters depends on the question being asked** — purpose, population, grain, time window, identifier scheme. Two columns can agree on type, key-ness, and notation and still refer to different populations; two that agree on none of them can refer to the same one.

This is the sharpest available argument against "we normalize your data into one model," and it is worth landing in copy.

## 5. The guide and answer engine — how a question is answered

The consumption side, and the part that most shapes how we should describe the product. Six stages; the LLM appears at exactly two (`20260723214551`).

| Stage | Actor | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Interpret | **LLM** | question → goal(s) from a closed catalog; structured output, schema-validated |
| 2 · Ground | engine | terms → map identities; ambiguity surfaced, never guessed |
| 3 · Assess | engine | answerable / partial / refused; missing pieces named |
| 4 · Execute | engine | fixed plan per goal type → answer object with full input trace |
| 5 · Narrate | **LLM** | answer object → prose; adds nothing |
| 6 · Deliver | log | goal + answer object + narration shown together, all logged |

The Sinai docs record these as **driving reasons** rather than decision statements — the arguments to re-weigh if a decision is ever challenged. The load-bearing ones:

**Translate and narrate only** (`20260723214615`). The LLM interprets intent and presents results; its output is never the source of a fact. The measured basis: semantic-layer-mediated answering benchmarks at **98–100%** versus **84–90%** for free-form query generation (dbt, 2026), and the gains came from constraint quality, not model size. The sharper argument is failure *shape* — generation fails silently-plausible, selection fails loudly out-of-scope — and studies of multi-step agent failures attribute **~75%** to plausible-looking wrong outputs that trip no failure signal. Note also: schema validation constrains form, not semantics. A well-formed wrong query passes every check.

**Closed goal catalog** (`20260723214616`). Every answerable question shape is an enumerated goal type: a parameter schema, a fixed execution plan, an answerability predicate. Growth is an engineering change, not a prompt edit. This is what turns "translate the question" from generation into selection — and what makes refusal mechanically computable rather than a matter of LLM judgment.

**Ambiguity surfaces, never auto-resolves** (`20260723214647`). Grounding returns resolved, ambiguous-with-candidates, or unknown. Grounding — not query logic — is where NL-to-graph systems empirically fail; alias mismatches yield empty or wrong result sets that *look like answers*. And ambiguity is often real information: the same term genuinely naming different things in different layers is a finding about the data estate, not noise.

**Refusal is a complete answer** (`20260723214648`). Assessed before execution, with the missing pieces named. Three reasons worth carrying into copy: one plausible answer to an unanswerable question outweighs many correct ones; named gaps are productive output that direct enrichment exactly where consumer demand hit a wall; and refusing early is also the cheap path.

**Answerability is not map-completeness** (`20260811195247`). An owner clarification worth quoting internally: strictness is about whether *the asked question* can be answered, not whether the map is complete in every adjacent fact. Conflating the two turns every incomplete annotation into a refusal of a still-answerable question, and the calibrated-trust promise collapses into "refuse unless perfect."

**The engine concludes nothing of its own** (`20260811215523`). It gathers assertions already in the map and arranges them into the shape the goal asks for. Where the map holds nothing about two things, the answer says so. An engine filling that gap would produce claims with no author, no evidence, and no confidence — arriving in the answer indistinguishable from claims that have all three. Correspondingly, correspondence between layers is **relayed from what writers asserted, never derived from structure** (`20260811215554`).

**An answer is a route, never data** (`20260812212315`). The map stays external to the data path: the engine says what to query; running it stays with the consumer. Answers are accurate only on the current map — like a pathfinding result — so they are never cached or stored. If the question matters tomorrow it is asked again, and having the map is what makes re-asking cheap. Freshness therefore needs no tracking of its own.

**Translation vs. conclusion** (`20260817210659`). The boundary on model output runs between restating a claim the map holds and adding one it does not. Rendering the map's understanding of a join *as SQL* is translation — every table, column, and join condition traces to an assertion. Saying that query is efficient or safe to run is a new claim no writer made. **The form of the output settles nothing.**

**Delivery is three artifacts side by side** (`20260723214649`) — goal, mechanical answer object, narration — plus a replay log. Each catches a failure the others cannot: narration drift is detectable only against a shown answer object; misinterpretation (answering the wrong question correctly) only from the shown goal. Note the finding that apparent confidence rises with compute rather than correctness — heavier retrieval produces more confidently-wrong prose — which is why the raw mechanical output stays visible.

**The narrator sees the claim side, not the audit trail** (`20260817210717`). Gaps are load-bearing in what the narrator sees, not incidental: a narrator that cannot see what the map does not hold will write around the hole, and the join it invents to do so is the most costly failure available to a trust product.

**Labeled exploratory mode** (`20260723214733`). Questions outside the catalog route to a plainly-labeled ungrounded mode carrying no mechanical-confidence claims. This escape valve is what lets the catalog stay closed — without it, coverage pressure forces premature goal types. Exploratory traffic doubles as the demand signal for which goal types to build.

**Consumer-supplied model** (`20260817210708`). A customer integrating the map into their own agent skips narration and takes the mechanical answer object directly. The answer object is a deliverable in its own right, not an internal stage.

### Built engine implementation

Bespoke, in Elixir, inside the hub (`20260723214711`) — not an adopted reasoner. The July 2026 survey: the Hex datalog package is abandoned; Soufflé and Clingo are batch-oriented C++ CLIs; Datalevin and XTDB are JVM residents; ProbLog and Markov Logic implementations self-classify as research software. Beyond maintenance, an external engine means a second store, and mirrors drift — answers must be computed against the live map. The closed catalog removes the need for generic entailment, which was the one thing external reasoners uniquely offer. A standing revisit trigger is recorded: if goal types emerge needing recursive rule chaining to fixpoint, embed CozoDB behind a context module rather than finish the accidental Datalog evaluator.

## 6. Capabilities and the reasoning ladder

Capabilities are decomposed because the monolithic annotator was the wrong unit of evolution (`20260625215309`): **fitness masking** — a blended scalar averages away per-capability signal — and **capability bleed** — one shared LLM call means a prompt change for one capability physically alters all others' outputs. Both are structural, not measurement artifacts. External backing: GEPA (per-module prompt evolution, 4–35× sample efficiency) and KARMA (multi-agent KG decomposition, +4.6–14.4% accuracy).

Each capability declares `name`, `rung`, `reads`, and `uses_llm`, with its evolvable payload (prompt, model route, tool allow-list) as a plain dict in `artifact.py` so one optimizer shape targets any capability.

**Dependency is a ladder of reasoning, not a run-order graph** (`20260814175258`). The question determining dependency is which annotations let an annotator *reason better*, never which capability happened to execute first. Rungs split where the kind of machinery changes. Rung order is then reused as the staleness cascade: a capability goes stale only from ingests or lower rungs — never same-rung siblings, never its own writes — so new evidence propagates upward without a separate cascade engine (`20260817232647`).

**The pipeline is incremental, not cold-start** (`20260622220225`). Evidence accumulates as first-class permanent content; capabilities re-apply to affected entities as new evidence arrives — a new DDL file, a user correction, an ontology update.

**Concurrency, honestly stated.** A single LLM annotation run takes on the order of **1–20 minutes** (`20260804173624`). Several runs may work one source at once, and because capabilities hold no local copy they are concurrent writers on shared state (`20260804173645`). Resolved by a per-run staged scope whose writes join the map at completion (`20260804175907`). Re-run convergence is the stated **aim, explicitly not an invariant** — an LLM's output varies between runs (`20260804175928`).

## 7. Evaluation

Two measurements, and the distinction is sharp.

- **Annotator fitness** holds the ontology fixed and varies the annotator.
- **Ontology evaluation** (`20260810215351`) holds the annotation fixed and varies the ontology, reading what a consumer stops getting without a given property.

The most consequential recent finding (2026-08-13 to 08-17) is **lesion tautology** (`20260817210427`): read at a deterministic engine, the difference a removed property produces merely restates the code that consumed it. So the difference must be read at something that **reasons from the property's content** — the model at interpret and narrate (`20260817210438`). This is also the answer to whether a meaning-carrying property can be measured at all: presence is the whole signal a deterministic reader can see.

**DP-Bench was chosen over text-to-SQL** for exactly this reason (`20260817210629`). DP-Bench scores answering against a curated data product *and* against raw schema, and reports the gap — a published demonstration that its scores are sensitive to what a catalog carries. A plain text-to-SQL benchmark scores query correctness against a fixed schema, so a richer or poorer catalog leaves the score untouched and an ontology change has nowhere to show up. DP-Bench's shape also matches the product's: a request for the data serving a stated need is a routing question, and the gold data product is the routing answer.

**Benchmark maps are produced by the real pipeline, run blind to the gold** (`20260817210542`) — not hand-authored, not taken from the dataset's own descriptions.

**Schema Genesis** (`20260624212149`) supplies the estates: four LLM roles (Business, Designer, Implementor, Narrator) with deliberately limited cross-role visibility simulate a dysfunctional-but-recognizable organization over N sprints, maintaining three parallel diagram silos at different abstraction levels. Output carries accretion, inconsistency, and emergent structure, plus a supporting-evidence menagerie — API docs, payloads, exports, guides, scraped pages, FAQs — so benchmark inputs are realistic multi-modal evidence environments rather than bare schemas.

The University of Utah Phase I sub-award (Notion) turns this into a public benchmark suite: schema-to-schema mapping, semantic joinability, granularity/unit/temporal alignment, healthcare generalization as primary tests; semantic Q&A, entity resolution, blast-radius, and **correct refusal scored as a success** as secondary.

## 8. Built vs. target — the claim boundary

This is the section to check copy against.

### Built and living

- The hub: single internal API, MCP adapters, job orchestration, push-launch runner, ingestion
- EAV/triple persistence in Postgres
- Layers, including composite layers and scope-bound references
- The assertion-evidence model: existence assertions, corroboration, one evidence mechanism, computed provenance
- Deterministic structural derivation plus LLM enrichment capabilities on a rung ladder
- The guide loop and the answer engine, with a closed goal catalog including `concept_anchoring` (physical footprint)
- Labeled exploratory mode
- Cross-layer correspondence as relayed writer assertions
- The DP-Bench evaluation harness and Schema Genesis
- Automated derivation pipeline with coverage-gated dispatch and per-layer advancing mode

### Marked `status: target` — designed, not built

- **The calibrated confidence system** (`20260630163507`) — predictor/rejector, multivariate Platt calibration, conformal risk control, the confirmation flywheel
- **The learning system** / active-learning acquisition (`20260708170636`)
- **The elicitation system** — simulated respondent, document requests, human interfaces
- **`anti_label`** — structured negative space

### Say this precisely

The confidence values on the map today are **prototype-grade self-reported scores**. The engine deliberately computes **no confidence rollup** (`20260723214732`) — a deterministic fold over uncalibrated inputs would produce a number that *looks* mechanical while inheriting inputs the calibration work explicitly distrusts, which the docs call the most misleading possible artifact for a trust product. What an answer does carry is the **complete set of assertions it was computed from**, each with its evidence references.

So: we can say the system tracks what supports every claim and surfaces uncertainty and gaps. We cannot yet say it produces calibrated confidence scores, asks you the minimal set of questions, or learns from your answers. Those are the roadmap.

Also do not claim, per the existing brief guardrails and consistent with Sinai: fully autonomous schema maintenance, production-proven accuracy at scale, guaranteed cost or time reductions, elimination of data engineers, a universal clinical-semantic standard, or automatic access to patient records.

## 9. Point process and time series — read this before writing about it

**Finding: the built engineering contains essentially no point-process or time-series capability.** A full-text sweep of the 956 Sinai docs for point-process, time-series, and temporal terms returns only: Schema Genesis's multi-sprint temporal accretion (schemas evolving over time, not data), the retired Pathways wearable product, the Mauna Kea DuckLake/Parquet time-series exchange architecture, and incidental uses. There is no timeline assembly, no event-ordering goal type, no point-process modeling in FieldMap.

Meanwhile the strategy record pushes hard in that direction:

- The **revised SBIR aims** (Notion, 2026-08-23) make Phase I *"validation pipeline for downstream applications of FieldMap ontology for time series and point-process data"* and Aim 2 *"optimization of point-process timeline assembly."* An action item from 2026-08-20 is to sit with the new program officer to modify Phase 1 objectives **from time-series to point-process data**, on the finding that there is no strong market demand for time series.
- Market discovery found *"significant demand for a platform that turns diverse inputs into well-structured point-process data"* and that *"the primary annotation bottleneck is in the data engineering step with schemas and tables."*
- Warren's own framing: time series = continuous values sampled over time; point processes = events at different times. **EHR entries lag events by weeks, so building aligned timelines across sources is itself a hard data-engineering problem.** That sentence is the bridge between the two — the timeline is a data-engineering artifact before it is an analysis object.
- The Deep MTN session of 2026-08-13 named temporal/sequential goals as an important gap — *"Did event A occur before event B?"* where record insertion order differs from actual event order — and **logged them as phase two**. The chosen posture for temporal ambiguity is notable and honest: rather than inferring change points, explicitly record known states at known times and surface the unknown gap between them.

**What is genuinely adjacent and built:** `concept_anchoring` answers where a concept physically lives as a *footprint* across `part_of`; the correspondence vocabulary already carries grain and time-window as dimensions a claim is conditional on (`20260811215522`); `granularity / unit / temporal alignment` is a **primary** test in the U of U benchmark hierarchy; and the annotation vocabulary sketched in Notion's *Metadata Wanted* (valid-time vs. transaction-time, versioning pattern, temporal granularity and grain boundary, `captured_at_event`, freshness/staleness) is the natural target ontology — but it is a wanted list, not a shipped one.

**Messaging guidance.** Point process is a *direction* backed by market discovery and a grant, not a shipped capability. Write it as where the work is heading, or as the class of problem MTN understands, never as something Guide does today. And per the Sept–Dec hypothesis doc, temporal handling is deliberately positioned as a **technical-track differentiator**: it belongs in a CTO conversation, a technical appendix, or a demo — the doc's own expectation is that it does *not* improve top-of-funnel response as the lead pitch.

## 10. What to lead with

Grounded in the above, the claims that are both true and distinctive:

1. **Schema-first.** Guide works from schemas, documentation, API definitions, and synthetic or anonymized data — no PHI or raw records required to begin. This is real, load-bearing, and it lowers security and procurement friction. It is also the honest version of "we don't need your data."
2. **The map is external to the data path.** An answer is a route — what to query — never data results. Guide tells you where to look; running the query stays with you.
3. **Maintenance, not just mapping.** Layers localize change: a schema change re-evaluates one edge, not the estate. This is the difference between a one-time consulting deliverable and a subscription.
4. **Honest refusal.** "The map cannot answer this, and here is what is missing" is a complete answer, and those named gaps are the roadmap for what to annotate next. No competitor leads with this, and it is the most credible thing we have.
5. **Evidence under every claim.** Every assertion cites what it rests on, including a human's confirmation as first-class evidence. Competing claims coexist rather than being silently overwritten.
6. **Map, don't merge.** Equivalence between two systems' concepts is partial and conditional on the question being asked — so we record how far they coincide and under what conditions, rather than forcing a single unified model.

The through-line: **MTN Guide is a map you can trust because it tells you what it does not know.**
